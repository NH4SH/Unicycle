## Design Context

### Users
HoosFinds is for UVA students in Charlottesville who want a trusted, campus-native resale marketplace centered on clothing, personal style, and curated everyday finds. They are typically browsing on mobile between classes, around dorm move-outs, before game day, or while coordinating quick pickups on Grounds, on The Corner, or near off-Grounds apartments. Their main job is to discover and sell stylish, local, student-to-student finds quickly without the randomness and friction of GroupMe threads, Facebook Marketplace, or generic classifieds.

### Brand Personality
HoosFinds should feel stylish, editorial, youthful, local, and trustworthy. The emotional goals are confidence, discovery, taste, and ease. The product should feel like the stylish resale layer of UVA: fashion-forward and curated, but still approachable and student-native. Core personality words: refined, campus-native, fashion-first.

### Aesthetic Direction
The interface should be light-first and grounded in warm paper tones, near-black text, restrained UVA orange accents, and muted navy or cool blue support tones. Clothing imagery should carry the visual center of gravity, with generous whitespace, strong hierarchy, premium layout discipline, and minimal clutter. Use impeccable.style as inspiration only at the systems level for typography hierarchy, section rhythm, whitespace, and editorial restraint. The result should feel original to HoosFinds and unmistakably UVA-first. Anti-references: generic startup landing pages, Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist, chaotic thrift boards, overly corporate SaaS dashboards, loud neon gradients, and hackathon-style UI.

### Design Principles
1. Style leads, utility supports. Clothing, vintage, streetwear, outerwear, sneakers, accessories, and game-day fits should shape the visual hierarchy, with secondary campus categories orbiting around that core.
2. Be unmistakably UVA-native. Copy, pickup flows, and browsing cues should feel natural to students on Grounds without becoming gimmicky or mascot-heavy.
3. Prefer editorial clarity over interface clutter. Use typography, spacing, image scale, and composition before adding more cards, containers, or decorative UI.
4. Build trust without losing youthfulness. The product should feel safe, polished, and reliable while still feeling current, social, and desirable.
5. Keep motion subtle and purposeful. Interactions should feel smooth and responsive, with premium restraint rather than flashy gimmicks.
6. Stay mobile-first and accessibility-conscious. Critical actions must remain easy on small screens, with clear focus states, strong contrast, and reduced visual noise.
